## Why this request exists, and what it does *not* ask for

**This is a narrow request, and it is narrow because most of the record was already obtained
without asking.** The City publishes its council agendas and packets openly through PrimeGov
(`limaoh.primegov.com`), and the full text of Ordinance 198-26 *as introduced* — title, nine
recitals, Sections 1 through 5, the blank vote grid — is at pp. 154-156 of the 2026-09-14 meeting
packet, now committed at `data/documents/lima/council/`. Nothing below re-requests it. A records
request that asks for what the agency already published invites a "see our website" response and
teaches the office that the requester has not looked.

What the open record does **not** contain is anything about what happened at the meeting:

- The City publishes **no Minutes document** for the 2026-09-14 regular meeting. PrimeGov returns
  meeting 359 with exactly three documents — Agenda, Packet, HTML Packet — all published 2026-09-10,
  four days *before* the meeting. The 2026-08-17 special meeting does have minutes (published
  2026-09-15), so the portal carries minutes when they exist; these have simply not been approved
  and posted. That is an expected lag, **not a refusal, and this request does not characterize it as
  one.**
- The committed copy of the ordinance is unsigned. It prints "Passed: \_\_\_\_, 2026" and "Approved:
  \_\_\_\_, 2026", and its vote grid (VOTE / 1ST / 2ND / 3RD, each Y|N, over GORDON, WILKERSON, LOWE,
  JORDAN, JONES, GLENN, NEEPER, DIXON) is entirely blank.

So the fact of passage, the tally, the mover and seconder, the reading, the effective date and
therefore **the end of the eighteen-month clock** are all unestablished. Items 1-3 close that.

Item 5 is different and is the largest hole in the Lima record generally: the **executed
water-service agreement** for the data-center campus in American Township. It is the contract that
Ordinance 198-26's own preexisting-contracts sentence turns on and that the Mayor's transmittal memo
characterizes, and this corpus does not hold it. The memo (2026-09-08, packet p. 43) is the only
document in the corpus that names it, and a memo describing a contract is not the contract.

Item 4 is included because it is cheap, bounded, and is the natural successor: the standards
ordinance is branch (1) of the moratorium's early exit, so the work product behind it determines
when the pause ends.

## Scoping notes (why the request is written the way it is)

Each item names an instrument type, a body and a date or date range so it can be answered
independently and a partial response is still useful. Nothing here asks for anything exempt on its
face: no personnel files, no attorney-client material, no security records, no trade secrets. Item 5
is the one item where an exemption is foreseeable — a developer may have asserted trade-secret
protection over commercial terms, as happened with the engine ratings in the companion Ohio EPA air
permit — so item 5 asks explicitly for a redacted copy with the withheld portions identified rather
than inviting a wholesale denial.

## A separate request, not drafted here

Resolution **#582-26**, the Second Amendment to the Sanitary Sewer Development Agreement (the
2026-07-30 sewer redesign), is an instrument of the **Allen County Board of Commissioners**, a
different agency. Appending it to a City of Lima request would give the City a reason to treat the
whole request as misdirected. It belongs in its own request to the Board's clerk.

>    that authorized the City to enter into it. If any part of this agreement is withheld as a trade
>    secret or under any other exemption, I ask for a **redacted copy** with the withheld portions
>    identified, rather than a withholding of the whole record.
>

> specific exemption relied upon, as R.C. 149.43(B)(1) requires. If any item above is ambiguous or is
> deemed overly broad, please contact me and give me the opportunity to revise it before denying it,

> these records so that I can revise the request accordingly.
>
> Please acknowledge receipt. I am happy to take these on a rolling basis as they become available
> rather than waiting for a complete response — in particular, items 1-3 may become available
> together once Council approves the September 14 minutes, and item 5 need not wait for them.
>
> Thank you for your time.
>
> Cory Parent

### Constructive-denial watch

Ohio has no fixed statutory deadline; R.C. 149.43(B)(1) requires production "within a reasonable
period of time." An unreasonable delay is treated as a **constructive denial** and is what makes a
mandamus action under R.C. 149.43(C) available. The corpus already carries worked examples of this
posture — see `data/extracted/legal/prr-mandamus/`.

⚠️ **Items 1-3 are subject to a legitimate, non-discretionary lag.** Council approves its minutes at
a later meeting; until it does, the approved journal does not exist and cannot be produced. Counting
that delay toward a constructive denial would be wrong and would poison a genuine claim later.
Item 5 is under no such constraint — it is an executed contract that exists today — and it is the
item on which delay actually means something.
